How RAG Champ's Annual Report Analyzer Adds Value

The same question, "Analyze Travelers' commercial insurance business from its 2025 10-K", was answered twice: once by a plain Claude Code session, and once by the RAG Champ Annual Report Analyzer. Both outputs were checked figure by figure against the filing. The plain session wrote the better business story. RAG Champ produced the deeper and better-verified financial analysis: tied out, page-cited, reproducible, and it found issues the other run missed. They are complementary, and the value of RAG Champ is that it gives every story a base of numbers that can be checked.

1. The Verdict in Short

RAG Champ is the better analyst
  • It went one level deeper into the numbers. It read the 18 pages of reserve-development triangles and found that about $440m of favorable workers' comp development was offsetting about $250m of adverse general-liability development in 2025.
  • It tied every segment figure to the consolidated statements (✓ marks), in three places for reserves.
  • It raised three real anomalies in the filing that the plain session did not mention: a $6m allowance gap, a $109m gap between incurred claims and the P&L, and an unexplained held-for-sale allocation.
  • Every figure carries a note and page citation, and the exact prompt Claude received is saved.
The plain session is the better storyteller
  • It used Item 1 (Business) and MD&A: how the segment is organized, distribution, state concentration, retention and rate trends, the catastrophe treaty, the Canada sale and Fidelis.
  • It quoted the company's reported combined ratio (91.7%). RAG Champ, using notes only, had to build an approximate 95.6% that is not comparable with what Travelers reports.
  • Its output is a finished playbook document with a bottom line.
  • Every narrative figure checked was correct. Only one phrase could not be confirmed (Section 8).

Opinion. On a famous company and a well-structured filing, a capable model with web access can write a good, mostly correct overview. RAG Champ's value is not that the plain model fails. It is that RAG Champ makes the numbers dependable, deeper, checkable and repeatable, the qualities a user needs before acting on an analysis or comparing companies. The best output combines the two: RAG Champ's verified figures and red flags, plus MD&A context, written up in the playbook format. That is the role this document recommends for RAG Champ inside the STOCK INVESTMENT PLAYBOOK (Section 6).

4. Where RAG Champ Adds Value

4.1 Claude sees exactly the right evidence, all of it

A 221-page 10-K is too long to read carefully in one pass, so a free-roaming session reads selectively. RAG Champ removes that choice. Deterministic Python cuts the filing into notes, and Claude picked five of them by title. The user then confirmed the five, and they were sent verbatim with their tables intact, including the full 18-page Note 8 with every accident-year triangle. The model never has to decide what to skim.

The result is visible in the reserve analysis. The plain session says workers' comp is "the steady source of reserve releases" and that general-liability losses "came in higher than expected". RAG Champ quantifies both from the triangles: about −$440m and +$250m. It shows AY2022 general liability rising from 1,696 to 2,014 (+18.8%) since the first estimate. It then concludes that BI's favorable development "depends on workers' comp releases while liability lines strengthen", at the same time as workers' comp premiums shrink 3.2%. That is the kind of finding an investor pays for.

4.2 Every number is tied out, and the checks are visible

The prompt carries all five primary statements, plus the LINKED FIGURES that connect statement lines to the notes (from the XBRL tags) and the CHECKS (26/26 ok from the calculation linkbase). Claude is required to explain the impact on every statement. That produced a reconciliation the plain session never attempted:

Premiums 43,914: three segments sum exactly ✓ · Claims reserves 65,737 = BI 51,655 + B&SI 5,672 + Personal 8,407 + Other 3 ✓ (Schedule III) = 67,643 + 3 − 1,909 held for sale ✓ (Note 8) · Net income 6,698 − 373 − 37 = 6,288 ✓

Tie-outs are how an analyst knows the segment story is consistent with the audited totals. With them, the reader can rely on the analysis instead of re-checking it.

4.3 It finds what does not add up

Because it reconciles, RAG Champ notices the gaps. Each one was confirmed in the filing (Section 8):

  • Reinsurance allowance: the balance-sheet caption says $129m (p.121), while Note 6's table shows $(135)m (p.155).
  • Incurred claims: Note 8's total of 27,112 (p.157) does not equal the P&L's 27,221.
  • Held-for-sale business: $4,550m of assets (p.121) and $1,909m of reserves sit in an unnamed segment. RAG Champ infers from the reserve movements that it is probably Personal Insurance (the Canada sale) and asks for confirmation.
4.4 Traceable, auditable, repeatable
  • Page citations on every figure, so anyone can open p.135 and check.
  • The exact prompt is saved (Claude-code-prompt-29-prompt.txt). The question "what did the model actually see?" always has an answer.
  • No tools, no browsing: Claude can only use the filing text it is given, which removes a whole class of errors (stale web pages, other years' filings, other companies).
  • The same method every time. The same extraction, prompts and checks give the same content for every company and every year, so analyses can be compared.
  • Follow-ups stay grounded. A follow-up continues the thread with the same notes and searches the filing again for anything new.
4.5 It scales to any filer in seconds

The XBRL route read three filers it had never seen: Nvidia, The Hartford and Travelers. Each ingested in 1–3 seconds with no setup, every automatic check passed, and 74–81% of statement lines were linked to their notes. A plain session has to find and read each 10-K again every time. RAG Champ reads it once into a package, and every later question costs only the analysis.

5. Where the Plain Session Did Better

5.1 The business, not just the accounts

The plain session's sections on how the segment is organized and its strategy notes come from Item 1 and MD&A. That covers the five markets and their share of premiums, independent-agent distribution, California at 13.2% of premiums, the catastrophe treaty ($575m above a $2.89bn retention), large-deductible receivables of $3.01bn, the US$2.4bn Canada sale to Definity, and the 20% Fidelis quota share. All of it checked out, and none of it is in the notes RAG Champ was given. For a "business" question this context matters as much as the numbers.

5.2 The company's own KPIs

Travelers reports a Business Insurance combined ratio of 91.7% (p.66). RAG Champ's approximate 95.6% divides all of Note 2's G&A, including costs that serve fee income, by premiums. It said so, but a reader comparing with Travelers' investor materials would be confused. Retention, renewal-premium change and the underlying combined ratio are also MD&A figures. A notes-only analysis cannot quote them.

5.3 A finished document

The plain session produced a playbook page with a clear thesis ("close to its peak in this pricing cycle"), strengths and watch items, and the two numbers to track each quarter. RAG Champ's report is a working paper. It is more rigorous, but it needs another step to become a story.

6. What This Means for the User

A user building conviction in a stock needs three things from an annual-report analysis. The test above shows where each comes from:

To see what others missHeadline-level readingAccident-year reserve trends and filing inconsistencies, from the full notes
The recommended place for RAG Champ in the playbook

Use RAG Champ as the evidence engine and the story agent as the writer:

  1. Download and ingest the filing on the XBRL page (seconds, no setup).
  2. Run both RAG Champ modes on the question. Identify notes gives the numbers, tie-outs and red flags. Analyze non notes gives business context and the reported KPIs from MD&A.
  3. Hand both answers to the story agent, with the rule to use only these figures and keep their page citations, and have it write the playbook page (CIRCULAR-AI style).

The playbook page then has the plain session's readability and the analyzer's verified depth. Every figure in it traces back to a page of the filing, and the analysis can be repeated next quarter or on a peer (CB, HIG, TRV) in the same form.

7. Gaps to Close in RAG Champ

#Gap seen in this testSuggested change
1A business or segment question was answered from the notes only, without the reported KPIs or business context.Add a combined mode that sends the chosen notes plus the top MD&A segment passages (the segment's "Results of Operations"). At minimum, step 3 should suggest "Analyze non notes" when the question names a business or segment.
2An approximate ratio (95.6%) stood in for the reported one (91.7%).Add a prompt rule: when the extract contains a ratio the company reports, quote it, and label any derived ratio as derived with its basis. A combined mode would supply the reported ratios.
3The output is a working paper, not a playbook page.Add an "Export to playbook" step: the story agent turns one or more saved RAG Champ answers into the playbook HTML format, keeping the page citations.
4Time and cost were not measured for the other approach.RAG Champ already logs timings and prompt sizes. Record the token cost too, so future comparisons are complete.
5A single reader's judgment.Repeat the comparison on 3–5 more questions and companies (reserves at CB, debt at JPM, tax at NVDA) before drawing firm conclusions.
