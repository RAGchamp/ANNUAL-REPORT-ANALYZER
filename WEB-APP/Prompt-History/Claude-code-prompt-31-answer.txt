# Risk on Hartford's corporate bond portfolio (31-Dec-2025)

## Summary

- **The corporate book is the largest single credit exposure.** It is **$23,076m at fair value**: 50.1% of AFS bonds, 36.1% of total investments and **1.22× stockholders' equity** (Note 5, p.150; Balance Sheet, p.125).
- **The risk shown so far is mainly interest-rate (mark-to-market) risk, not credit loss.** The net unrealized loss narrowed from $(916)m to $(229)m. The ACL on corporates fell to **nil**, and the company booked no net credit losses on AFS in 2025 (Note 5, p.149–150).
- **Aged losses remain.** $7,089m of corporates have been under water for 12 months or more, with $(570)m of losses. That is 94% of the corporate unrealized losses (Note 5, p.151).
- **Valuation risk is rising.** Level 3 corporates grew to **$2,866m** from $2,281m, mostly private placements priced on unobservable spreads (Note 4, p.141–142, 145–146).
- **Key data is missing from the material**: credit-rating mix (investment grade vs. below), duration, and sector or industry split beyond the "financial services 11%" remark. Credit quality therefore cannot be fully assessed.

## 4. Valuation / Level 3 risk (Note 4, p.141–146)

- **Hierarchy split for 2025:**
  - Level 2: $20,210m (87.6%).
  - Level 3: $2,866m (12.4%; 2024: 11.1%).
  - Level 1: none.
- **Level 3 roll-forward** (Note 4, p.146):
  - Movement: 2,281 + 3 (net income) + 63 (OCI) + 1,402 purchases − 842 settlements − 91 sales + 62 transfers in − 12 transfers out = 2,866 ✓.
  - Growth is driven by purchases of private placements, not by transfers.
- **Unobservable inputs:** $2,765m is valued by discounted cash flows. Spreads range from 88 to 811 bps, with a weighted average of **289 bps** (2024: 286 bps on $2,166m) (Note 4, p.145).
  - The remaining $101m (2,866 − 2,765) is broker-priced and excluded from the input table.
  - Wider spreads lower fair value.
- **Exposure to below-investment-grade debt:** the inputs table mentions "below investment grade privately placed securities and private bank loans" (Note 4, p.144). The amount is not given.

## 5. Concentration (Note 5, p.150)

- **Issuers:** the largest are Morgan Stanley, SPCC Funding I LLC and Entergy, each under 1% of invested assets. No single issuer exceeds 10% of equity.
- **Sectors:** financial services is the largest sector at about 11% of invested assets. Measured against total investments, that is roughly 0.11 × 63,957 ≈ $7.0bn. This is an estimate, because "invested assets" is not defined in the material.

## 6. Synthetic corporate credit (Note 6, p.157, 160)

- **Exposure:** $392m notional of **below-investment-grade (B+) corporate basket CDS** sold, 2 years to maturity, fair value $27m.
- **Offset:** the exposure is fully offset by $392m of offsetting swaps (fair value $(27)m). Net economic exposure is therefore nil, leaving counterparty risk. Collateral is posted.

## Impact on the financial statements

- **Income Statement:** corporate credit losses would flow through **net realized losses**, which were $(100)m in total (P&L, p.123; Note 5, p.148). The corporate ACL release of $3m was a small benefit. Net investment income from fixed maturities was $2,367m, but the material does not split corporates out (Note 5, p.148).
- **Balance Sheet:** $23,076m sits inside "Total fixed maturities, AFS" of $46,041m (Balance Sheet, p.125), at fair value. There is no ACL deduction in 2025.
- **Other comprehensive income / Shareholders' Equity:**
  - Corporates contributed a pre-tax improvement of about **$687m** (−229 vs. −916). That is about 60% of the $1,141m total AFS improvement behind the $898m OCI gain (Comprehensive Income, p.124).
  - At the ~21% implied tax rate, that is roughly $543m after tax.
  - It reduced the AOCI deficit, which moved from $(2,886)m to $(2,057)m (Equity statement, p.126).
  - Level 3 corporate OCI was +$63m (Note 4, p.146).
- **Cash Flow:** purchases and sales of corporate bonds sit inside the AFS lines: purchases of (12,769) and proceeds of 10,352 (Cash Flow, p.127). They are not split by sector.

## Red flags / follow-up

1. **No rating, duration or industry breakdown** for the corporate book is in the material. Ask for the NAIC/rating mix and the below-investment-grade exposure (needed to judge both the nil ACL and the Level 3 privates).
2. **$570m of losses aged 12 months or more** depend on the hold-to-recovery assertion. A forced sale (for liquidity or capital) would move them into earnings.
3. **Level 3 private credit is growing fast** (+25.6%, from 2,281 to 2,866). It is valued with internal matrix and broker inputs, which carries model risk.
4. **Spread sensitivity:** no sensitivity is disclosed. A widening of spreads would reverse part of the 2025 OCI gain.
5. **Financial-sector concentration** of about 11% of invested assets is correlated risk; two of the three largest issuers are financial.
