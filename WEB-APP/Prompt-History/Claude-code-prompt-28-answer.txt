## Summary

- **Tax liabilities on the balance sheet more than doubled.** Current taxes payable, long-term income tax payable and long-term deferred tax liabilities total **$8,401m vs $3,955m (+112%)** (Note 9, p.68). This is faster than the 92% rise in tax expense.
- **The main driver is uncertain tax positions.** Gross unrecognized tax benefits (UTBs) rose **54.5% to $4,420m** (Note 13, p.76), and accrued interest and penalties rose to $374m. Long-term income tax payable, which is "primarily" UTBs, rose 81% to $3,958m.
- **Gross deferred tax liabilities tripled to $4,611m.** The drivers are equity investments (+$1,963m), in line with the $8.9bn equity-security gains, and unremitted foreign earnings (+$922m). Because deferred tax assets grew more, the company stays in a **net deferred tax asset position of $11,484m**.
- **The effective tax rate rose from 13.3% to 15.1%.** The causes are a smaller stock-compensation benefit, a heavier foreign mix (Israel) and the first-year adoption of a new disclosure format. The 15.1% is still well below the 21% statutory rate, mainly because of the foreign-derived deduction eligible income (FDDEI) benefit, the Israel incentive rate and tax credits.
- **Cash taxes ($20,288m) lagged current tax expense ($22,807m) by $2,519m.** This accrual build is consistent with the rising payables. All note figures tie to the statements.

## Detailed analysis

### 1. Composition of tax liabilities

| Liability | Nature | FY2026 | Share of total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Income tax payable, long-term | Mainly UTBs plus interest and penalties | 3,958 | 47% |
| Taxes payable, current | Taxes due within one year | 2,669 | 32% |
| Deferred income tax, long-term | Net deferred tax liabilities by jurisdiction | 1,774 | 21% |

- **Weight in total liabilities:** tax liabilities are 8,401 / 49,510 = **17.0%** of total liabilities (FY2025: 3,955 / 32,274 = 12.3%) (Balance Sheet, p.53).
- **Current taxes payable tripled.** This matches current tax expense running ahead of cash payments: 22,807 − 20,288 = **$2,519m accrued but not yet paid**.

### 2. Uncertain tax positions (the most judgmental part)

- **Additions:** current-year additions were **$1,959m**, up from $1,268m (+54.5%). They equal about 8.6% of current tax expense (1,959 / 22,807).
- **Releases:** lapses, decreases and settlements total 224 + 157 + 76 = **$457m**.
- **Rate impact:** $3.7bn of the UTBs would lower the effective tax rate if recognized. That is about 2.6 percentage points of FY2026 pre-tax income (3,700 / 141,450).
- **Interest and penalties:** the expense was $103m vs $92m.
- **Open examinations:** these cover the US, Germany, Hong Kong, India, Israel and Taiwan, for fiscal years 2014–2025 (Note 13, p.76).
- **Reconciliation to the balance sheet:** UTBs plus interest are 4,420 + 374 = **$4,794m**, but long-term income tax payable is **$3,958m**. The gap of about $836m is presumably UTBs netted against deferred tax assets or classified as current. This is not disclosed.

### 3. Deferred taxes

**Growth in deferred tax liabilities:**
- Equity investments rose from $264m to $2,227m. This is consistent with the $8,918m equity-security gains (Cash Flow, p.55) and with non-marketable equity securities rising from $3.4bn to $22.3bn (Balance Sheet, p.53).
- Unremitted foreign earnings rose from $891m to $1,813m, reflecting the growth in foreign earnings (foreign pre-tax income $18,269m vs $6,570m, +178%).

**Deferred tax assets:**
- Net CFC tested-income assets rose by $2,569m.
- Accruals and reserves rose by $1,586m, in line with the warranty and inventory reserves in Note 9.
- Capitalized R&D fell by $820m, consistent with the OBBBA enactment (Note 13, p.74).

**Valuation allowance:** $711m of state valuation allowance was released. This lifts the quality of the deferred tax assets.

**Netting check:** balance-sheet deferred tax assets of $13,258m minus the long-term deferred tax liability of $1,774m = **$11,484m** ✓. For FY2025: 10,979 − 886 = 10,093 ✓.

### 4. Rate drivers (FY2026 reconciliation, Note 13, p.73)

**Statutory starting point:** 21% × 141,450 = $29,704m ✓.

**Main reductions:**
- FDDEI: (4,208)
- Israel reduced rate: (3,064)
- Credits: (1,933)
- Stock-based compensation: (1,475), vs (2,097) in FY2025

**Main additions:** Israel other (+1,606) and other foreign jurisdictions (+741).

**Arithmetic checks:**
- FY2026 items sum to **$21,383m** ✓.
- FY2025 sums to 11,146 ✓ and FY2024 to 4,058 ✓ (p.74).

**Effective rate by origin of income:**
- Foreign: (2,550 + 825) / 18,269 = 18.5%.
- US: (19,039 + 1,218 − 1,364 − 885) / 123,181 = 18,008 / 123,181 = 14.6%.

**Cash tax rate:** 20,288 / 141,450 = **14.3%** (FY2025: 15,118 / 84,026 = 18.0%).

## Red flags & areas for follow-up

1. **Rapid UTB growth.** UTBs rose 54.5% to $4.4bn, with $1.96bn of current-year additions and audits open across six jurisdictions since FY2014. Ask management which positions are involved (transfer pricing? FDDEI? Israel?) and what range of outcomes they expect.
2. **UTBs and the long-term payable do not reconcile.** $4,794m of UTBs plus interest compares with $3,958m of long-term income tax payable. Ask for the classification: netted against deferred tax assets, or current.
3. **Payables vs the current-tax accrual.** The payables increase of $3,558m exceeds the $2,519m current-tax accrual. Ask what explains the roughly $1.0bn difference.
4. **Movement in net deferred tax assets.** Net deferred tax assets rose by $1,391m, but the deferred tax benefit was $1,424m, a gap of $33m. The likely causes are acquisitions or OCI, but this is not disclosed.
5. **Acquisition deferred taxes.** Goodwill rose $15.6bn and intangibles $2.5bn, yet the acquired-intangibles deferred tax liability *fell* from $70m to $38m. Ask whether the acquisitions were asset deals or tax-deductible.
6. **Indefinite reinvestment.** About $1.4bn of foreign earnings is indefinitely reinvested, with an unquantified unrecorded deferred tax liability.
7. **Comparability.** The new reconciliation format was adopted prospectively, so FY2026 categories (e.g., "Israel – Other +1,606") are not comparable with FY2025's "foreign tax rate differential".
8. **Rate sustainability.** The stock-comp benefit is falling, and the OBBBA effects should be monitored.
9. **No inconsistencies or typos found.** Every table cross-foots and ties to the statements.
