## Summary
- **What "commercial" covers:** Travelers has no line called "commercial insurance". I have used its two commercial segments: **Business Insurance (BI)** and **Bond & Specialty Insurance (B&SI)**. Together they earned **$26,519m of premiums in 2025 (60% of the $43,914m group total)** and produced **$4,645m of segment income (69% of the $6,698m total)** (Note 2, p.135).
- **Profit grew faster than premiums.** BI segment income rose **+11.8% to $3,695m** and B&SI rose **+16.6% to $950m** (Note 2, p.135). The drivers were higher net investment income (BI +8.7%), more favorable prior-year reserve development, and a lower loss ratio.
- **BI's underwriting margin is thin and investment income carries it.** BI's approximate combined ratio is **95.6%** (2024: 96.4%). Net investment income of $2,782m is about **61% of BI's pre-tax income**. B&SI is the high-margin business, at an approximate **82.4%** combined ratio.
- **Growth is slowing.** BI net written premiums grew only **+2.7%**, against **+5.0%** for earned premiums. Workers' comp and National Property were flat or down. Growth came from commercial auto and commercial multi-peril (CMP).
- **Reserve risk sits in liability lines.** The development tables show **adverse development in BI general liability of about +$250m in 2025** (accident years 2018–2023) and asbestos additions of **$277m**. Both were masked by about **$440m of favorable workers' comp** development (Note 8, pp.158, 161, 165, 173).

## Detailed analysis

### 1. Underwriting ratios
These are approximate ratios calculated from Note 2 (p.135), each line divided by earned premiums. G&A includes costs that serve fee income, so this is not the company's reported expense ratio.

| Ratio | BI 2025 | BI 2024 | B&SI 2025 | B&SI 2024 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Loss & LAE | 14,154/22,412 = **63.2%** | 13,679/21,345 = 64.1% | 1,764/4,107 = **43.0%** | 1,774/3,958 = 44.8% |
| DAC amortization | 3,796/22,412 = 16.9% | 3,588/21,345 = 16.8% | 778/4,107 = 18.9% | 756/3,958 = 19.1% |
| G&A | 3,482/22,412 = 15.5% | 3,303/21,345 = 15.5% | 843/4,107 = 20.5% | 832/3,958 = 21.0% |
| **Approx. combined** | **95.6%** | **96.4%** | **82.4%** | **84.9%** |
| Catastrophe ratio | 1,073/22,412 = 4.8% | 1,032/21,345 = 4.8% | 0.6% | 1.3% |
| PYD benefit | (233)/22,412 = (1.0)% | (90)/21,345 = (0.4)% | (5.4)% | (3.3)% |

**BI underlying loss ratio** (excluding catastrophes and prior-year development):
- 2025: (14,154 − 1,073 + 233) / 22,412 = **59.4%**
- 2024: (13,679 − 1,032 + 90) / 21,345 = **59.7%**

The underlying ratio improved only about 0.3 points. Most of BI's 0.9-point loss-ratio gain came from prior-year development, not from current-year pricing.

### 2. Pre-tax rebuild of segment income (ties to Note 2)

- Less tax 891 = **3,695** ✓
- Effective tax rate: 891 / 4,586 = 19.4%

**BI 2024**
- 24,657 − 20,570 = 4,087; less tax 781 = **3,306** ✓
- Effective tax rate: 19.1%

**B&SI 2025**
- 4,579 − 3,385 = 1,194; less tax 244 = **950** ✓

**Where BI's pre-tax income comes from (2025)**
- Underwriting result: 22,412 − 21,432 = 980

Net investment income is therefore **61%** of BI's pre-tax income (2,782 / 4,586). This reflects BI's long-tailed reserves: reserves are **2.3×** earned premiums (51,655 / 22,412), which creates a large float (Note 20, p.219).

**Interpretation:** BI's reported favorable development depends on workers' comp releases while liability lines strengthen. Workers' comp premiums are shrinking (−3.2%), so this cushion may fade.

## Red flags & areas for follow-up
1. **Allowance mismatch.** The balance sheet caption shows an allowance for uncollectible reinsurance of **$129m**, but the Note 6 table shows **$(135)m** (BS p.121 vs Note 6 p.155). The difference is $6m, possibly the held-for-sale portion. Clarify.
2. **Held-for-sale business.** Assets of $4,550m and liabilities of $2,542m are held for sale (BS p.121), including $1,909m of reserves (Note 8, p.157) and $285m of recoverables (Note 6, p.155). The provided notes do not say which segment holds this business.
   - Personal Insurance reserves fell from 8,992 to 8,407 although its premiums grew, which suggests the business is in Personal Insurance.
   - Confirm that the commercial segments are unaffected.
3. **Adverse liability development.** BI general liability is about +$250m adverse in 2025, AY2022–2023 keep rising, and B&SI general liability AY2024 is up 7.2%. Ask about social inflation and the adequacy of reserves for recent accident years.
4. **Recurring asbestos charges.** Additions were $284m (2023), $242m (2024) and $277m (2025), and management says further liabilities "cannot now be reasonably estimated" (Note 8, pp.173–174).
5. **Dependence on workers' comp releases.** About $440m of favorable development came from workers' comp in 2025, while workers' comp premiums are falling.
6. **Slowing written premiums.** BI NWP grew +2.7% against +5.0% earned. Ask about retention and rate trends by market.
7. **Small tie-out gap.** Note 8's total incurred of 27,112 does not equal P&L claims of 27,221 (a difference of 109). This is likely accident & health, held-for-sale or other items, but it is not explained.
8. **Expense allocation basis.** Schedule III expenses are allocated on statutory practices (Note 20, footnote 2), and net investment income is allocated notionally (Note 2, p.134). Segment margins therefore depend on allocation choices.
