JPMorgan Chase: exposure to mortgage-related assets (FY2025 vs FY2024)

Summary

  • The exposure is about $683bn, or 15.4% of total assets, and it shrank slightly. It has four parts: mortgage-backed securities (MBS), retained residential mortgages, commercial real estate (CRE) loans and mortgage servicing rights (MSRs). Together they fell from $696.4bn to $682.8bn (−2.0%), while total assets grew 10.5%. The decline came from held-to-maturity (HTM) MBS run-off (−$11.5bn) and lower residential mortgages (−$6.0bn). CRE loans grew by $3.1bn.
  • Credit quality is strong on average, but late-stage delinquencies are rising. The weighted-average loan-to-value (LTV) is 48% and the weighted-average FICO score is 775. However:
  • residential nonaccrual loans rose 21.7%;
  • loans 150 or more days past due rose 60%;
  • loans modified for borrowers in financial difficulty ("FDMs") rose roughly fivefold, from $206m to $1,024m. This was mostly forbearance after the January 2025 Los Angeles wildfires, and $571m of these FDMs were 150+ days past due.
  • The biggest market risk is an unrealised loss on HTM MBS that is not in equity. HTM MBS have a fair value $9.9bn below cost. Because HTM securities are carried at amortised cost, this loss never passes through earnings or accumulated other comprehensive income (AOCI). The unrealised loss on available-for-sale (AFS) MBS, which does go through AOCI, narrowed from $3.9bn to $1.1bn.
  • MSRs are stable at $9.2bn and earnings from mortgage fees are small. Mortgage fees and related income was $1,381m, under 1% of total net revenue. Hedging gains from MSR risk management are getting smaller.
  • CRE: the risk sits in office, retail and industrial lending. For "Other commercial" CRE, 9.75% of loans are criticized and nonaccrual loans rose to 2.08%.

All HTM securities are investment grade, and about 99% are rated AA+ or better (p.233). - Duration and yield. AFS MBS yield 4.55%, but HTM MBS yield only 2.87% (p.234). The HTM book is older, low-coupon paper bought before rates rose. Substantially all residential MBS mature contractually after 10 years. Their expected weighted-average lives are about 7 years for agency MBS, 6 years for agency collateralised mortgage obligations (CMOs) and 4 years for non-agency CMOs (p.234, footnote c). - Unrecognised loss. HTM MBS have a net unrealised loss of $9.9bn, which is 2.7% of stockholders' equity (9,922 / 362,438). Across all HTM securities, the loss is 270,134 − 253,344 = $16.8bn, or 4.6% of equity. - AFS → HTM transfer. In Q3 2025, $44.1bn of securities moved from AFS to HTM, carrying $575m of pre-tax gains in AOCI (p.230). The note does not say how much of this was MBS. - Credit allowance. The allowance on all investment securities is only $106m (p.233), and the provision was a release of $(28)m.

2. Residential mortgages ($303.5bn retained)

  • The quality of the book is high:
  • weighted-average LTV is 48% and weighted-average FICO is 775;
  • loans with LTV above 100% total only $362m (71 + 4 + 282 + 5);
  • $296.4bn of loans have LTV at or below 80% (p.245).
  • Concentration is high. California is 38.7% of the book (117,500 / 303,531). New York adds 15.3%.
  • Delinquencies are building at the late stage:
  • 30+ days past due rose from 2,214 to 2,571 (+16%), and the ratio rose from 0.71% to 0.84% (p.244);
  • 150+ days past due rose 60%;
  • nonaccrual loans rose $648m.
  • Why charge-offs fell anyway. Charge-offs dropped to $58m because these loans are well collateralised and wildfire forbearance delays any charge-offs.
  • FDMs. Of the $1,024m, $882m are second 90-day forbearances for borrowers hit by the Los Angeles wildfires. The rest extended loan lives by 19 years and cut the weighted-average rate from 6.94% to 6.08% (p.246).
  • Other figures:
  • loans in foreclosure: $575m (p.246);
  • Q1 2025: of the $1.0bn of FDMs, $571m were 150+ days past due (p.246).

3. Commercial real estate ($165.5bn)

  • Multifamily ($105.1bn) is the larger and cleaner part: 4.43% criticized and 0.40% nonaccrual.
  • Other commercial ($60.4bn), mainly office, retail and industrial, is weaker: 9.75% criticized and 2.08% nonaccrual, up from 1.80% (p.255).
  • CRE gross charge-offs rose from $324m to $420m (+29.6%) (p.253).
  • Construction and development loans were $12.4bn (p.255).
  • CRE FDMs rose from $354m to $815m, and $438m of these are criticized nonaccrual. Defaults of FDMs within 12 months of modification jumped from $3m to $173m (p.259). This is the clearest stress signal in the mortgage-related book.

4. MSRs and mortgage banking

  • MSR movements. The MSR asset of $9,167m is the result of:
  • additions of $1,066m;
  • run-off ("collection/realisation of expected cash flows") of $(1,068)m;
  • valuation changes of +$48m.

Third-party loans serviced rose to $668bn (p.276). - Interest-rate sensitivity is limited: - a 20% adverse change in prepayment speed would cut fair value by $(353)m; - a 200bp adverse change in the option-adjusted spread would cut it by $(757)m (p.277).

Both are small next to equity. - Revenue. Production revenue was flat at $622m. Net servicing revenue fell from 751 to 704, as risk-management gains dropped from 159 to 118 (p.277).

Red flags & areas for follow-up

  1. Unrealised HTM losses of $16.8bn in total ($9.9bn on MBS) are invisible in equity. Ask how much of the $44.1bn moved from AFS to HTM was MBS, and why the transfer was made.
  2. Residential FDMs rose fivefold and loans 150+ days past due rose 60%. These are concentrated in California (38.7%). Ask about the outcome of the wildfire forbearances after the second 90-day period, and about expected losses.
  3. CRE FDM defaults rose from $3m to $173m, and Other-commercial CRE nonaccrual loans rose to 2.08%. Ask for the office exposure separately.
  4. Two small inconsistencies in Note 15. MSR run-off is $(1,068)m in the MSR activity table (p.276) but $(1,065)m in the mortgage fees table (p.277). For FY2024 the figures are (1,068) and (1,067). Ask for the reconciling item.
  5. Loans at fair value in Consumer, excluding credit card, more than doubled, from 15,531 to 33,183 (p.241). These are probably mortgages held in CIB, but the type is not disclosed.
  6. Total-row label is ambiguous. In Note 10, "Total investment securities, net of allowance $777,360" is the total amortised cost (507,226 + 270,134). The Balance Sheet figure of 777,332 uses AFS at fair value. Both are correct, but the label could mislead readers.
