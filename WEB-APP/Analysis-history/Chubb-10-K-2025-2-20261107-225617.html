Summary

  • North America Agricultural Insurance is Chubb's smallest P&C segment, at 5% of 2025 consolidated NPE (Item 1, p.7). The audited figures agree: $2,919m of $53,014m is 5.5% (Note 19, p.206).
  • The segment has two businesses (Item 1, p.7–8):
  • Rain and Hail: federally subsidised MPCI crop insurance plus private crop-hail cover.
  • Chubb Agribusiness: farm and ranch cover, and commercial agribusiness P&C.
  • 2025 was a strong year:
  • Net premiums earned rose 7.9%.
  • Underwriting income rose 46% to $517m.
  • Segment income rose 40% to $577m (Note 19, p.206–207).

What management says about the business

  • Market position: management states Chubb has been one of the leading U.S. crop insurers since the 1980s. It works through Rain and Hail, a managing general agent subsidiary, and is geographically diversified across U.S. crops (MD&A, p.78).
  • MPCI:
  • The program is run with the USDA's Risk Management Agency, which sets the terms, rates and forms (MD&A, p.78).
  • It covers revenue (commodity price and yield), yield, margin, prevented planting and replant (Item 1, p.7).
  • Deductibles range from 10% to 50% (MD&A, p.78).
  • Risk is shared with the government through the Standard Reinsurance Agreement (SRA), which has pro-rata and state stop-loss terms. Chubb adds third-party proportional and stop-loss reinsurance, and may use crop derivatives (MD&A, p.78).
  • Crop-hail: a private product. Chubb sets its own rates and limits exposure through township liability limits and reinsurance (MD&A, p.79).
  • Competition: all approved MPCI providers sell the same product forms and rates, so management says Chubb competes on service and digital tools (Item 1, p.8).
  • Economics and seasonality:
  • Premiums depend on February commodity prices and on price volatility.
  • Losses depend on the October harvest price and on crop yields.
  • Written and earned premiums are concentrated in Q2 and Q3.
  • The underwriting result is usually not estimable until Q4 (MD&A, p.79).
  • Outlook: the 2026 SRA has no significant changes, so management says it does not affect the 2026 outlook (MD&A, p.78). This is an expectation, not a result.

2025 performance

The MD&A states (p.58):

  • Net premiums written rose $223m (8.2%), mainly because of a $179m favourable year-over-year effect from the federal profit-share agreement.
  • Higher acreage and policy growth also helped, partly offset by lower commodity prices.
  • Net premiums earned rose $214m (7.9%).
  • The combined ratio fell because of lower catastrophe losses, partly offset by less favourable prior-period development.

The $179m adjustment is most of the NPW growth. Without it, NPW grew about $44m (223 − 179), or about 1.6% of 2,703. The adjustment itself is not disclosed in the notes provided.
